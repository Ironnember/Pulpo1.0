# Pulpo Campaign — The Friction Is the Feature

## Status

**Proposed marketing campaign.** Messaging must remain bounded by current reproducible evidence. This document creates no authority, policy, permit, executor, router, ledger, or production claim.

## Core idea

Most AI products compete to remove friction from action. Pulpo exists to put the right friction back between intelligence and consequence.

The campaign position:

> The friction is the feature.

Pulpo should occasionally be the system that says no, asks for proof, rejects stale authority, blocks a replay, refuses a broadened scope, or forces reconciliation before learning becomes policy.

That is not degraded intelligence. It is governed consequence.

## Primary doctrine

> Intelligence proposes. Governance disposes. Execution obeys. Evidence reports.

## Campaign thesis

AI is becoming better at deciding what to do and increasingly capable of doing it. Capability alone does not establish authority.

Pulpo is the governance and evidence plane between intelligence and consequential execution. Its job is not to make the model smarter. Its job is to determine whether a proposed action is actually authorized, bind that authorization to the exact permitted work, deny invalid or stale authority, preserve evidence, and reconcile what happened afterward.

## Evidence-backed claims available now

Current canonical repository evidence supports messaging around these behaviors:

- unknown, incomplete, and over-budget intents fail closed;
- selected high-impact actions require verifier-backed approval;
- one-use permits are bound to exact intent and cannot be replayed;
- replay, permit, approval-ID, nonce, and audit state can persist across restart in the canonical kernel;
- persisted audit-chain tampering fails closed on restart;
- configured agent roles cannot exceed their action, resource, or cost grants;
- bounded commerce state preserves reservations, attempted orders, reconciliation, and spend across restart;
- governed directive activation/revocation has an executable proof path in which ordinary chat/retrieval cannot create authoritative directives, retrieval relevance cannot elevate authority, and revocation persists across restart.

## Claims we do not make yet

Do not market Pulpo as production-ready or as already proving:

- independently deployed human signing authority;
- trusted verifier bootstrap in production;
- rollback-proof protected host storage;
- real payment-rail enforcement;
- network isolation;
- hostile-code sandboxing;
- distributed identity;
- broad external reproduction across independent operators.

## Launch sequence

### Phase 1 — Establish the problem

Message: AI capability is outrunning authority architecture.

Content:
- capability is not authority;
- successful execution is not proof of legitimate execution;
- memory, retrieval, or model confidence must not silently increase permission.

### Phase 2 — Introduce the constitutional separation

Message: separate intelligence, governance, and execution.

Content:
- intelligence proposes;
- Pulpo resolves authority and policy;
- execution receives only narrowly bound permission;
- evidence is reconciled afterward.

### Phase 3 — Show denial proofs

Message: governance is credible when it can prove what it refuses.

Content demonstrations:
- replay denial;
- over-budget denial;
- broadened-scope denial;
- revoked directive denial after restart;
- retrieval/model text attempting to elevate authority and failing.

### Phase 4 — Consequential proof

Message: one bounded real-world action, fully governed end to end.

Target sequence:

`request -> discover -> quote -> authorize -> execute -> verify -> reconcile -> learn`

Publish only after the real execution and evidence boundaries are satisfied.

## First public post

**The friction is the feature.**

Everyone is racing to make AI agents faster, more autonomous, and easier to deploy.

Pulpo is being built for the moment after that becomes consequential.

The model can reason. It can plan. It can recommend an action. But capability is not authority.

Pulpo separates intelligence from governance and execution. A proposed action has to survive identity, authority, policy, budget, approval, and permit checks before consequence. Then the result has to reconcile back into evidence.

That means Pulpo should occasionally be irritating.

It should reject a replay.
It should refuse stale authority.
It should block a broadened scope.
It should ignore a model confidently declaring itself approved.
It should remember what happened without allowing memory to silently become policy.

If Pulpo never tells us no, we probably built it wrong.

**Intelligence proposes. Governance disposes. Execution obeys. Evidence reports.**

## Campaign invariant

Marketing may explain verified behavior and clearly labeled proposals. Marketing may not transform an inference, branch experiment, planned integration, or successful demo into a production claim.

The campaign itself follows Pulpo doctrine:

**Evidence first. Claims second.**
